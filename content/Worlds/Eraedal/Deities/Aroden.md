# Aroden: The Slumbering Architect

> [!QUOTE]
> "The thread of humanity is not cut; it is merely stretched across the quiet dark. Let the outer worlds declare our throne vacant. We shall maintain the masonry of empire, enforce the law of the stone, and fuel our altars with his lingering sparks until the Architect awakens."
> — *The Testament of the Last Azlanti, High Imperial Liturgy*

Aroden is the patron deity of human civilization, history, culture, innovation, and grand manifest destiny. Once a mortal hero of the ancient world who ascended to godhood by raising the Starstone from the heart of the Inland Sea, he stands as the foundational, absolute anchor of the Western Empire’s **[[The Imperial Rite]]**. While outer planar realms and distant continents claim that Aroden died or utterly vanished—shattering the cosmic web of absolute prophecy and bringing forth the chaotic Age of Lost Omens—the core orthodoxy of the Western Empire rejects his death entirely. Within your campaign world of Eraedal, Aroden is treated as a "Slumbering" or "Muted" entity whose fading cosmic remnants still serve as a highly transactional power grid for the authoritarian human state.

## Divine Profile

### Core Mechanics
* **Titles:** The Last Azlanti, The Slumbering Architect, The God of Humanity, The Guide of Culture, The First Emperor
* **Areas of Concern:** Human civilization, innovation, history, preservation of culture, and manifest destiny.
* **Divine Font:** *Heal*
* **Divine Attribute:** Intelligence or Charisma
* **Divine Skill:** Society
* **Favored Weapon:** Longsword
* **Divine Sanctification:** Can choose Holy
* **Domains:** Cities, Duty, History, Justice

### Devotee Benefits
* **1st-Level Spells:** *Command*
* **4th-Level Spells:** *Discern lies*
* **7th-Level Spells:** *Divine decree*

### Edicts & Anathema
* **Edicts:** Build and structurally fortify cities, public roads, and aqueducts; meticulously document family bloodlines, civic census data, and accurate historical ledgers; advance human craftsmanship, engineering, and architectural sciences; enforce the legal statutes of recognized sovereign monarchs.
* **Anathema:** Abandon a civilized human settlement or public work to barbarism or wild decay; intentionally falsify, forge, or censor an imperial historical or genealogical record; allow extra-planar entities or unstructured "folk" traditions to subvert municipal law.

## Appearance & Iconography

Before his modern muting, Aroden was traditionally depicted as an idealized, statuesque Azlanti patriarch possessing flawlessly symmetrical features, long dark hair bound in silver bands, and calm grey eyes that held the collective memory of a thousand kingdoms. He is almost always shown fully clad in ornate, gleaming gold-and-steel plate mail, holding a heavy marble tablet detailing building schematics in his left hand and a pristine, unsheathed longsword in his right. 

In the modern visions of the Imperial Church, he appears more abstractly: a colossal silhouette encased in solid, white-hot marble armor, motionless upon a crystal throne while his face remains permanently obscured by a thick, amber-colored solar fog.

His primary holy symbol is a stylized winged eye pointing upward toward a crown of twelve stars, or a pristine silver longsword wrapped in a legal scroll parchment. Sacred animals include those associated with urban nobility, structure, and endurance, such as lions, white eagles, and thoroughbred horses.

## The Eraedal Matrix: The Slumbering Spark

Unlike standard canonical settings where Aroden is an absolute dead memory, the Western Empire of Eraedal functions on a far more complex, high-stakes theological reality. **Aroden's magic has not been entirely extinguished.**

### The Divine Vestiges
Within the grand cathedrals of the Empire, the connection to the First Throne is visibly operational. **Several of the Church's highest-ranking High Priests, Prelates, and Inquisitors still successfully cast complex divine spells, channel holy energy, and execute *Heal* fonts directly utilizing the frequency of Aroden's name.** The Church weaponizes this tangible magic as their ultimate, undisputed proof of a "Divine Mandate" to govern the Tarldet Plains and command foreign enclaves.

Rogue magical scholars, alchemists, and elven traditionalists (such as *Calebrimbor*) harbor a far more cynical, academic interpretation. They believe that Aroden is indeed spiritually brain-dead or missing, and that the Imperial Church is effectively conducting "divine grave-robbing." Skeptics argue that the High Priests are not receiving grace, but are instead using forgotten, unstable magic to aggressively siphon residual energy out of massive **Ancient Azlanti Ley-Line Anchors** buried deep within the earth or tapping the fading kinetic battery cells hidden inside historic imperial relics. Regardless of the true source, the power is real—and it gives the Church a terrifying, bureaucratic monopoly over civil religion.

## Historical Shadow: The Ghost of the Edict

The modern behavior of Aroden's priesthood is heavily warped by a defining, paranoid historical event that occurred exactly 60 years ago according to the Ptolus timeline: **The Edict of Deviltry**.

Following a catastrophic surge of unstructured magic use, extra-planar summonings, and rogue cultist uprisings that threatened to fracture the Western Empire's domestic control lines, the Church enacted a sweeping, permanent decree. The Edict mandated a total, state-monitored registry for all arcane and alchemical practitioners, placed non-human faiths under strict human legal oversight, and aggressively centralized all religious authority beneath the Imperial Rite.

While the open inquisitorial purges of that era have long since concluded and modern colonial hubs like **Borlon** have drifted into a more integrated, mercantile reality, the **ideological spirit of the Edict remains fiercely alive within the Church**. Traditionalist priests and old-guard inquisitors still view unregistered magic as a form of inherent civic treason. They treat elven and dwarven faiths as unrefined, second-class "folk superstitions" that must be heavily policed to prevent another historical breakdown of order.
