## 🏛️ Overview: The Prince of the Radiance

**Prince Kirian Ylestos** is the preeminent religious diplomat, ecclesiastical statesman, and high prelate of the coastal metropolis of **[[Ptolus]]**. As the presiding sovereign of the monumental **[[Cathedral of the First Radiance]]** and a permanent ruling voting member on the plutocratic **[[Council of Coin]]**, Kirian occupies the most delicate and lucrative intersection of divine faith, civic politics, and high finance in the eastern [[Western Empire]].

Unlike his father—Holy Emperor [[Rehobath Ylestos]], who arrived in Ptolus as an exiled sovereign following the sack of [[Caer Itom]] in **702 IA**—Kirian has been a fixture of the city since **682 IA**. Dispatched east nearly four decades ago to establish a permanent patriarchal see on the coastal frontier, Kirian spent decades weaving church investments into shipping cartels, merchant guilds, and the ten noble houses.

Where his father burns with apocalyptic zeal to reconquer the Western Empire under the banner of the dead god [[Aroden]] and the authoritarian **[[Imperial Rite - Pantheon | Imperial Rite]]**, Kirian is an aristocratic pragmatist. He understands that the populace of Ptolus adores the benevolent, living divinities of the global **[[Order of Light - Pantheon | Order of Light]]**—[[Sarenrae]], [[Iomedae]], [[Abadar]], and [[Erastil]]—and deeply resents heavy-handed imperial theocracy. Kirian’s life's work is a masterful high-wire act: outwardly venerating his imperial father while quietly shielding Ptolus’s commerce and divine institutions from suicidal crusades.

1. **Managing the Holy Father:** Kirian welcomed his father and the fleeing curia to Ptolus in 702 IA, helping consecrate the Holy Palace. Yet Kirian privately dreads Rehobath's fanatical desire to conscript Ptolus's youth and bleed its treasury to march across the war-torn [[Middle Kingdoms]]. Kirian constantly works to delay, soften, or redirect his father’s more extreme imperial edicts.
2. **The Council of Coin Alliance:** On the city's ruling council, Kirian regularly votes alongside patricians like [[Dered Abanar]]. Kirian recognizes that adventurers pouring into [[Delver's Square]] and merchant fleets docking in the [[Bay of Ptolus]] generate the gold that sustains the Church's vast charitable clinics, monasteries, and paladin garrisons.
3. **The Shield of the Radiance:** Within the Cathedral, Kirian maintains orthodox devotion to the four pillars of the Order of Light. Under his administration, the church runs orphanages, hospitals, and consecrated morgues that prevent undead plagues from rising out of the [[Necropolis]].

## 🎭 Persona & Roleplaying Guide

- **Voice & Demeanor:** Cultured, silky, impeccably courteous, and possessed of effortless political gravitas. He speaks in measured sentences, always giving the impression that he knows more about your finances and family history than you do.
- **Key Philosophy:** *"Faith without coin is a candle in a gale; coin without faith is a vault without a lock. We build the stone that shelters both."*
- **Tension Point:** When pressed between the secular demands of Commissar [[Igor Urnst]] and the holy ultimatums of Holy Emperor [[Rehobath Ylestos]], Kirian uses procedural council delays, bureaucratic audits, and quiet diplomatic compromise to prevent open warfare in the streets.

## 🧭 Story Hooks for Adventurers

1. **The Dangerous Audit:** Prince Kirian suspects that high-level prelates loyal to his father are siphoning cathedral gold to fund illegal black-market firearms from the [[Shuul]] for the western war. He hires discreet adventurers to audit the Holy Palace treasuries.
2. **The Relic of the First Dawn:** A priceless relic from the dawn of Sarenrae's worship has been uncovered in the upper levels of [[Dwarvenhearth]]. Kirian needs the party to secure it before agents of House Vladaam or the underground chaos cults desecrate it.
3. **Council Intermediary:** When the [[Delver's Guild]] threatens to strike over increased temple resurrection tithes, Kirian recruits the party to broker a clandestine compromise between guild leadership and the church curia.
